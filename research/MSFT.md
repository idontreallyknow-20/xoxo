## What the company does
Microsoft sells cloud infrastructure (Azure), productivity software (Microsoft 365, Teams), operating systems (Windows), business applications, LinkedIn, GitHub and Xbox. Azure and Microsoft 365 are the engines; AI services now run through both.

## Why it is on the list
Compounder bucket. Rank 15 on quality. Forward P/E of 21.6 against a four year median of 35.8, the lowest multiple in three years, with Azure growth accelerating to 43% in the June quarter. The stock is only 8% off its high, so this is a quality at fair price idea, not a bargain.

## Business quality
Distribution moat: Microsoft 365 is the default in most enterprises, Azure is one of three hyperscalers, and the company owns the biggest AI distribution channel through Copilot. Fiscal 2026 Azure revenue passed $100 billion, up 41%. Free cash flow fell to $15.8 billion in a recent quarter from $20.3 billion a year earlier as capex rose 70% to $41 billion for the fiscal year. Insider ownership is negligible; management's track record on cloud is the best in large cap tech.

## Valuation
At $510 and 21.6x forward earnings the stock is well below its own 35.8x median and below Alphabet (23x) despite faster cloud growth. Three year view: if EPS compounds 13% a year the stock reaches roughly $34 of EPS in fiscal 2029. Bear: 20x on $30 (capex crushes free cash flow, Copilot seats consolidate) is $600, modest upside because the starting multiple is already low. Base: 26x on $34 is $880. Bull: 30x on $36 is $1,080.

## Last two earnings reports
Fiscal Q4 2026 (July 29, 2026): revenue $90.0 billion vs $87.6 billion expected, EPS $4.74 vs $4.24 expected, Azure up 43% in constant currency vs 40% the prior quarter. Stock rose 8% because management raised capex plans and cited demand. Fiscal Q3 2026 (April) showed the same pattern: beat, higher capex, mixed reaction. The debate is entirely about whether $190 billion of capex earns a return, not about current demand.

## The bear case
Microsoft is spending roughly $190 billion on capex, up 61%, and depreciation will weigh on reported earnings for years. Forty five percent of the $625 billion commercial backlog is tied to OpenAI, and OpenAI investment losses ($3.1 billion in one quarter) flow through Microsoft's income statement. If AI demand slows or hyperscaler pricing gets competitive, revenue decelerates while costs keep growing. Copilot has had branding and interoperability complaints and could lose seat share to cheaper agents. A 2026 bear target of $340 assumes the multiple stays compressed while free cash flow stays messy.

## Key risks and thesis killers
Thesis killers: Azure growth under 30% while capex keeps rising, OpenAI restructuring that reduces committed Azure spend, or free cash flow margin under 20% for a full year. Price trigger: a close under $400.

## Verdict
Buy, in the October tranche. Buy zone $465 to $525. Conviction 4 of 5.
