## What the company does
Intuitive Surgical makes the da Vinci surgical robot and the Ion lung biopsy system. Hospitals buy or lease the systems, then pay per procedure for instruments and service, which is most of the revenue.

## Why it is on the list
Compounder bucket, rank 67. Forward P/E 30.6 vs a 72.3 median, 39% off its high, while procedures grew 16% last quarter. The first credible competitors in twenty years arrived, and the multiple halved.

## Business quality
Razor and blade model with 11,710 da Vinci systems installed, up 12%, and surgeons trained on it for a career. Gross margin around 68 to 70%. Cash and investments $8.6 billion, no debt. Competition: Medtronic's Hugo (approved late 2025) and Johnson & Johnson's Ottava (cleared for ten general surgery procedures). Oppenheimer notes workflow problems for Ottava and footprint constraints for Hugo.

## Valuation

## Last two earnings reports
Q2 2026 (July 16, 2026): revenue $2.89 billion up 19%, procedures up 16%, 468 da Vinci systems placed vs 395, non-GAAP EPS $2.80 vs $2.19. Full year da Vinci procedure growth guided to 13.5% to 15.5%, gross margin 68 to 69% including a 1 point tariff hit. Shares fell 3.3% after hours despite the beat. Q1 2026 also beat with the same competition overhang.

## The bear case
For two decades the bull case rested on having no competitor; now there are two, one of them built into the operating table with half the footprint. Hospitals under budget pressure will use the alternatives to negotiate down the $1,800 to $3,200 per procedure instrument cost. Even after the drop the stock is 30x, which prices in continued mid teens growth that competition will erode.

## Key risks and thesis killers
Thesis killers: da Vinci procedure growth under 10%, system placements down year over year for two quarters, or instrument pricing cuts disclosed on a call. Price trigger: $290.
